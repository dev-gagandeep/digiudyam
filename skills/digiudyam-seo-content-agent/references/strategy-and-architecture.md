# SEO strategy and site architecture

Read this reference for opportunity analysis, page decisions, topic clusters, URL changes, and internal linking.

## Repository inventory

Inspect App Router and Pages Router routes, dynamic segments, content registries, static generation, canonical and metadata utilities, sitemap and robots generation, JSON-LD, breadcrumbs, navigation, footer, related content, CTA links, redirects, middleware, and protected paths.

For site-wide work, classify routes as public/indexable, public/noindex, or private/disallowed.

## Page decision framework

Evaluate search intent, SERP page type, DigiUdyam relevance, business value, conversion path, current coverage and authority, semantic overlap, cannibalization, unique evidence, and maintenance burden.

- **Improve:** an existing page satisfies the same intent and can become more useful.
- **Create:** intent is materially distinct and the page can provide unique value.
- **Merge:** pages compete for the same intent without distinct value.
- **Retain:** the proposed change offers insufficient benefit or evidence.
- **Change URL:** a material architecture, relevance, or usability benefit justifies migration risk.

Do not create pages for spelling variants, singular/plural variants, or city names with interchangeable copy.

## Topic clusters

Maintain Website Development, Local SEO, Google Business Profile, Digital Marketing, CRM, Automation, Industries, and Locations clusters.

For each page define its cluster, role, parent, breadcrumb, distinct intent, non-overlap statement, supporting and sibling pages, and conversion destination.

Supporting pages should link to their pillar where useful; pillars should expose helpful supporting pages. Cross-cluster links must follow a reader need, not a quota.

## URL strategy

Prefer short, stable, lowercase, hyphenated paths. Preserve established URLs unless change is justified.

Before changing a public URL, check available evidence for index status, canonical selection, landing-page performance, backlinks, rankings or impressions, internal links, sitemap entries, redirects, and chains.

For an approved URL change:

1. Map each old URL to the closest relevant new URL.
2. Use a permanent server-side redirect; avoid chains and mass homepage redirects.
3. Update every controllable internal link.
4. Set a self-referencing canonical on the destination.
5. Update sitemap, breadcrumbs, metadata, hreflang if present, and structured-data URLs.
6. Keep the redirect long-term and verify response and destination.

## Location and industry pages

Require a distinct user need and enough real inputs to be useful. Differentiation may come from genuine service availability, customer problems, regulations, workflows, examples, local contact information, or industry decision guidance.

Avoid doorway pages, city-name swapping, unsupported local presence, and thin service-industry combinations. Consolidate when unique value cannot be established.

## Internal linking engine

For every target page define:

- **Incoming:** source pages, placement context, and varied descriptive anchor purpose
- **Outgoing:** pillar, related service, industry/location context, educational support, trust page, and conversion page where useful
- **Removal/update:** obsolete links, redirects, misleading anchors, and duplicate paths

Prioritize usefulness. Avoid exact-match repetition, site-wide link inflation, orphan pages, and irrelevant links.
